Message

03 · Messaging
Confused people do not buy. Resonance over persuasion.

THE HERO AND THE GUIDE

Gandalf does not carry the ring. Frodo does. Your client is the hero; you are the guide who has been down the road before. The mistake that costs the most is casting yourself as the hero — fifteen years of experience, three qualifications, here is my philosophy. All true, all pointed at the wrong person. A reader is looking for themselves, and when they find your credentials instead, they respect you and they leave.

A GUIDE NEEDS BOTH OF THESE

EmpathyI understand where you are. This is what section two of your sheet is for
AuthorityI can get you out of it. Your training, your years, and the results you have already achieved

FIRST WHAT IS TRUE, THEN WHAT YOU SAY

Do not start writing copy. Start by working out what is actually going on for this person, in plain terms — ten lines under each heading, everything down before you judge any of it. The wording comes later, and it comes out of that. Get what is true down first; the words are a job for afterwards.

THE THREE LEVELS OF A PROBLEM

ExternalWhat tangible problem or challenge is the hero facing? This is the level that gets you read — and it is where almost everybody stops
InternalHow does that external problem make the hero feel on a personal level? This is what makes somebody feel understood rather than guessed at
PhilosophicalWhat bigger picture or ethical issue is tied to the hero’s problem? This is what makes them care rather than just nod

AND THE FOUR THAT FOLLOW

Desired outcomeWhat they actually want instead — the presence of something better, not just the absence of the problem
ObstaclesWhat has stopped them so far, including everything they have already tried
Cost of inactionWhere this goes if nothing changes — three months, six months, a year, five years. The direction they are heading, not a prediction
Authority and trustWhy you, and why anybody should believe it

WHY THIS IS THE HOUR THAT MATTERS

Get these right and two things follow. Your messaging resonates, because you are saying their own situation back to them more clearly than they can say it themselves. And your program gets easier to build — you know exactly what it has to solve, so nothing goes into it that does not earn its place.

THE FORMULA

I work with [ideal clients] who [specific problem] so that they can [desired outcome] without [what they are afraid of losing]. One sentence, settled — not three you are still choosing between.

YOUR PROGRAM IS THE VEHICLE

They are here. They want to be there. Your program is what carries them across. Name both ends precisely and the offer explains itself.

IT HAS TO SOUND LIKE YOU

Your own energy, your own style, your own voice. If it could have been written by anybody it will be read by nobody, and the people on your list know how you talk. One discipline while you write: take the clinical vocabulary out. Presenting, ruminating, dysregulated, processing. Those words belong to the other lane and they tell a reader they are being assessed rather than understood.

THE ANNOUNCEMENT, AND THE TWO THAT FOLLOW IT

One message to everyone, told as news rather than a sale — so there is nothing to decline — ending in a question anybody can answer. Then two follow-ups and no more. The first goes three days later and asks the same question again — the easiest one to skip, and the one worth sending. The second goes ten days after the announcement, takes the pressure off and hands it back. Then you stop, and you stop warmly.

THE LINE TO REMEMBER

Everybody with a goal is the same. They know where they want to be, something is in the way, they have tried and stalled, and part of them has started to believe it might not happen. Your job is to make it feel possible, then show them the way.
