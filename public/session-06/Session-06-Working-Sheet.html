Therapy+ · Working Sheet · Session 06

Offer & Conversation

A sale should be the natural outcome of a meaningful interaction. Serve, don't sell.
Seven steps, numbered as in the video, in two sittings with a break between. Sitting one builds your offer and your price. In sitting two you build your call guide and your slides, plan for the concerns you'll hear, and say it all out loud. Every step has a worked example: the coach for tech start-up founders you've followed since Session 04. Start at least three days before our next call or weekly drop-in. Then we go through your sheet together and practice the conversation.

02Your price

Price the transformation, never your hours: charge by value. Your first three clients pay a founding price of at least $1,500. That's a floor, not a target. Add $500, and that's the number you quote: anyone who decides within 48 hours gets $500 off. After every three clients, your price goes up by $1,000. You can change it before your first client.
Example. Their evenings, their health and their best thinking hours, while the company needs them most. Left alone: another year of late nights, and the same Sunday-night promise to do better.

Sitting 2 of 2 · steps 4 to 7 · your call · about 100 minutes with slides or about an hour without, then about 15 minutes out loud

04Your call guide

One page you keep beside you on every discovery call: the whole conversation, in order. At the top is a short note saying the call is coaching, not therapy. Ours is only for inspiration: use any format you like. Send the note with every invitation.
  1. Download it. It opens in Word or Pages.
  2. Open: insert your agenda, in two or three sentences.
  3. Questions: turn your answers in section two of your Session 03 sheet into four questions. A to C give where they are now, D what they want instead, E what has stopped them, F what it costs if nothing changes. Each one opens a topic. Follow up on what they say, and you'll ask eleven or so without trying.
  4. Check the fit: who isn't a fit, and what you'll hand them on the way out. Something simple you make later is fine. If it's therapy they need, point them to a therapist other than you.
  5. Offer: your program, tied to what they told you. Write it for a typical client, and use their own words on the call. Then what they'll have by the end, and your price sentence. If they haven't asked the price by then, ask: “Would you like to know the investment?”
  6. Close: your two therapist answers. The first is in section one of your Session 03 sheet. The second is your warm no. Then the ask, with both endings: a yes, or a short call booked inside the 48 hours. Leave “I'm too busy” and “Other concerns” for step 6.
  7. Read it through once. Delete anything you won't say, but keep the note at the top.
  8. Before your first discovery call, not tonight: spend fifteen minutes on your state board's website. Open its laws and rules and search for “advertising”. Add anything the rules require to the note or your offer. Your offer is the Offer part of your guide, and your slides if you made them. Where the rules require something, they win over anything in your guide or slides. If they say nothing about it, change nothing.
  9. Before your first discovery call: set up a way to take payment, and a coaching calendar separate from your therapy one.

Haven't done these yet? Tell us which at our next call or drop-in, and we'll plan them.

06Concerns, and how you'll handle them

For each concern you expect: ask what they mean, acknowledge it, then ask a question that helps them find their own answer. Bring it back to the result they want. Never answer with results you can't show. Then put your answer to “I'm too busy” on your guide, and a few words for each of the others under “Other concerns”.
Example. “I'm too busy.” Ask: “You've told me how much this matters. How would you make sure you had the time?” · “It's a lot of money.” Ask: “What do you mean by a lot?” Then: “What would having your evenings back be worth to you?” · “I need to think about it.” Ask: “Of course. What would you like to think through?” If it's the money or the time, talk it through now. · “I need to talk to my partner.” Ask: “That makes sense. What do you think they'll ask?” Offer a short call with you both, about the program and the price only. · “Will this work for me?” Ask: “What makes you unsure?” Then: “What would get in the way, and how would you handle it?”

07Say it out loud

Run the whole call out loud once, start to finish, from your guide, with your slides up if you made them. Say your price, then hold the silence and count. Say your two therapist answers twice, and each answer from step 6 once. End it both ways: once with a yes, once with a short call booked inside the 48 hours. On real calls, the first time you say your price to someone, mark them as an offer made on your list. We practice it together at our next call or drop-in.

Ways to make yes easier

Ideas for step 3. Change the words to fit your program. Use one limit at most, and never add a second deadline: your 48-hour price already rewards a quick decision.
For exampleBefore you use it
A limit: founding placesThree founding places at this price.It's true: your price goes up after three clients. Only name a limit you will hold.
A limit: how many you take onI take on two new coaching clients a month.Only if it's true. It also protects your therapy caseload.

Common concerns

The concerns that come up most, and a question for each, so they find their own answer. Change the words to sound like you. Never answer with a discount, or with a result you can't show. Your two therapist answers are already on your guide.
What to askThen
“It's a lot of money.”“What do you mean by a lot?”Listen. Then ask what the result they described would be worth to them. Hold your price.
“I'm too busy.”“You've told me how much this matters. How would you make sure you had the time?”Let them plan it. Don't promise it's quick.
“I need to think about it.”“Of course. What would you like to think through?”If it's the money, the time or the fit, talk it through now. Still not yet? Book a short call inside the 48 hours.
“I need to talk to my partner.”“That makes sense. What do you think they'll ask?”Ask how they feel about it themselves. Offer a short call with you both, about the program and the price only. What they told you stays theirs to share.
“Will this work for me?”“What makes you unsure?”Then: “What would get in the way, and how would you handle it?” Never promise a result.
“Now isn't the right time.”“When would be, and what would be different then?”If they name a real date, say when your 48 hours end, and book a short call before then. After that, your quoted price stands.
“I could work it out myself.”“You could. How long have you been working on it so far?”Then ask what another year of working it out alone would cost them. Let them answer.
“I've tried something like this before.”“What happened, and what would need to be different this time?”If what they tried was therapy, don't compare or critique it. If it sounds clinical, use your referral answer. Otherwise, bring it back to how your program handles that.
