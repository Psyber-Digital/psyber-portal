04 · Program Structure
Think in journeys, not sessions.

YOUR PROGRAM IS A PATH

Your program is a step-by-step path from where your client is to where they want to be. Every part of it moves them along that path, and anything that does not comes out.

NINETY PERCENT PRACTICAL

Aim for ninety percent practical and ten percent theory. Theory supports action. It never replaces it.

THE PITFALL TO WATCH

A complicated program is not an effective one. The pull for a therapist new to coaching is to show everything you know. Aim for practical results, preferably within eight to twelve weeks.

SLIP — THE TEST FOR EVERYTHING

SimpleEasy to understand and easy to start. If it's complicated, they won't start it
LeanOnly what is needed, and nothing extra. If it's too big, they won't finish it
ImpactfulEvery part moves them toward the transformation. If it doesn't, it's information they could find online for free
PracticalThey can use it this week, in their own life. If they can't, they drift back to how things were

SLIP APPLIES TO EVERYTHING YOU BUILD

The programOne transformation, eight to twelve weeks
The structureFour to six modules, and three units at most in each
Every sessionOne objective, and one action they leave with
Every worksheetOne page they will actually fill in
Everything elseCards, slides, emails, resources. If a part fails any of the four tests, simplify it or remove it

THE THIRTEEN STEPS, IN THREE STAGES

1 · Ask itIf I was [your niche], how would I [your transformation]?
2 · The big questionsEverything that comes to mind. Your own knowledge first. No editing
3 · Keep the essentialsSLIP. Three to five usually survive
4 · Put them in orderThe order your client's change actually happens
5 · ModulesOne per question you kept, or per pair that answers the same need, plus Module 1: Plan, Mindset & Success Factors. A one-sentence outcome for each
6 · Detailed questionsInside each module. No editing
7 · Keep what mattersThree at most per module, using SLIP
8 · UnitsThree per module at most, each answering one question
9 · Read it throughSimplify anything a client would not understand the first time
10 · Key ideasOne per unit
11 · Possible resourcesWorksheets, guides, tools. List them
12 · Keep it simpleOne simple worksheet and one Principle Card per session. Anything more earns its place
13 · WeeksEight to twelve in total

CREATE, THEN REFINE

Steps two, six and eleven are for letting ideas flow. Write everything down and judge none of it. In the others, you choose what stays. Three, seven and twelve cut hardest. A specific, narrow question sets your brain to work on answering it, so the whole structure is built from questions.

COMMON MISTAKES

Too much content. No clear journey. Trying to teach everything. Copying a generic program. Overcomplicating the structure. Your job is to get your client results.

THE LINE TO REMEMBER

You don't need a perfect program. You need a clear, simple structure that works. Refine later. Improve with feedback. Keep moving.
