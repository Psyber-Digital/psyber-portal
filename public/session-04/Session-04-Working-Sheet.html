Therapy+ · Working Sheet · Session 04

Structure

Think in journeys, not sessions.
Thirteen steps, numbered the same as the video. We start wide and get narrower. Steps two, six and eleven are for letting ideas flow, so write everything down and don't edit. In every other step, you choose what stays. Bring all thirteen to our next call, done in three sittings with a night between each: steps one to five, six to nine, then ten to thirteen. Start at least three days before the call. There is a worked example above every box, and it is the same client all the way down. Send the sheet in at least 24 hours before our next call, and bring your announcement draft from the Message sheet.
Sitting 1 of 3 · steps 1 to 5 · the shape · about 45 minutes

01The transformation, as a question

Take your coaching statement from Session 03, the “I work with ___ who ___ so that they can ___” line. Turn it into a question: “If I was [your niche], how would I [your transformation]?” It puts you inside your client's situation and keeps you on the main thing.
Example. If I was a tech start-up founder, how would I effectively integrate my work and personal life, ensuring sustained productivity and enhanced personal well-being?

02The big questions

What does someone have to work out to reach that transformation? Write from your own knowledge and experience for about ten minutes, as your client would ask the questions, in everyday words. Put down anything that comes to mind and don't edit. If a gap shows up, look it up after this sitting.
The question
Example. How can work and personal life be effectively balanced? · What time management techniques maximize productivity? · Which well-being practices are most effective? · How can technology aid in work-life integration? · What role do personal relationships play in a founder's well-being? · How can health and fitness be maintained amid a busy schedule? · What stress management techniques are effective in high-pressure environments? · How do I cultivate a supportive personal network? · What tools help with prioritizing and managing tasks? · How can a routine support both personal and professional growth?
The question

03Keep only the essentials

Apply SLIP. Keep the questions your client can't reach the transformation without. If it isn't necessary, remove it. Three to five usually survive.
Example. Kept: 1 · How can work and personal life be effectively balanced? 2 · What time management techniques maximize productivity? 3 · Which well-being practices are most effective? 4 · How can technology aid in work-life integration? 5 · What stress management techniques are effective in high-pressure environments? Cut: relationships, health and fitness, a supportive network, task tools and routines. Each is worth knowing. None is essential to the transformation.

04Put them in order

No need to rewrite them. Put the numbers from step 3 in the order your client's change actually happens. What has to come first? What unlocks the next step? What builds momentum? If two answer the same need, put them together. They become one module.
Example. 2, 4, 1, then 3 and 5 together. Time comes first, because nothing else changes until the week is under control. Technology next, because the right tools make the balance possible. Then balance itself. Well-being and stress management merge into one module, because they answer the same need.

05Turn them into modules

Each number in your step 4 order becomes a module, and a merged pair is one module. A module is one stage of the journey, usually one to three weeks. Module 1 is always Plan, Mindset & Success Factors, so type that name in its row. The question you put first in step 4 becomes Module 2. Give each module a short name, not the whole question. Then give every module its outcome in one sentence. By the end of it, what can your client do? Four to six modules. Use + Add module if you need a sixth.
Example. Module 1 · Plan, Mindset & Success Factors: they're clear on the journey and committed to it. Module 2 · Optimizing Time for Peak Productivity: they plan each week around their three most important outcomes. Module 3 · Leveraging Technology for Work-Life Integration: their tools protect two focused blocks a day. Module 4 · Balancing Work and Personal Life: they hold one clear boundary between work and home. Module 5 · Cultivating Well-Being and Resilience: they have a daily routine that keeps them steady through a hard week.

08Turn them into units

Each question you kept becomes a unit, with a short title. Three units per module at most. Past three, a module starts to bloat. Module 1's are always the same: The Blueprint, The Required Mindset, Success Factors. Copy them into its row.
Example. Module 2 · Optimizing Time for Peak Productivity: Time Management Mastery · Productivity Goal Setting · Effective Time Tracking.

09Read it through and simplify

Read every module and unit title from top to bottom. Simplify anything a client wouldn't understand the first time, and cut anything that repeats. Make the changes in steps 5 and 8. Use the box to note what you changed.
Example. “Productivity Goal Setting” became “Setting Your Weekly Goals”. “Effective Time Tracking” became “Tracking Your Time”. Plainer words, and a founder understands each one the first time.
Sitting 3 of 3 · steps 10 to 13 · key ideas, resources and weeks · about 40 minutes

10One key idea per unit

For each unit, the one idea it delivers. One line each. For Module 1, write what your client needs to know about the journey, the mindset it takes, and what makes clients succeed. One idea per unit keeps each module from growing too big.
Example. Module 1 · The Blueprint: how the twelve weeks fit together. · The Required Mindset: balance is a skill they can learn, like any other. · Success Factors: one small action between sessions, every week, even a busy one. Module 2 · Time Management Mastery: the first two hours of the day go to the most important work. · Setting Your Weekly Goals: three outcomes a week, set every Monday morning. · Tracking Your Time: one honest week of tracking shows where the hours really go.

12Keep it simple

The standard for each coaching session in your program, usually one a week, is one simple worksheet and one Principle Card. Anything beyond that has to earn its place, so for most modules the answer here is none. You'll build them later, one or two sessions ahead of your client.
Example. Every session: one worksheet and one Principle Card. Module 1: none. Module 2: the Time Management Planner as well, because they will use it every day. Modules 3 to 5: none. The worksheet carries it.

Build terms

The words this session borrows, in one place. Knowing which level you are working on is what stops the program growing into a syllabus.
The transformationWhere your client starts and where they end up. Written as a question, because a question has an answer and a topic does not
A big questionSomething your client has to work out to reach the transformation. Each one you keep becomes a module
A moduleOne stage of the journey, usually one to three weeks. Four to six of them, and Module 1 is always Plan, Mindset & Success Factors
Its outcomeOne sentence: by the end of this module, the client can… A module without one does not have a job yet
A detailed questionA question inside one module. Each one you keep becomes a unit
A unitOne piece of a module, answering one question. Three per module at most. A unit is a piece of content, and one session can cover one or more of them
A key ideaThe one thing a unit delivers. One per unit
SLIPSimple, Lean, Impactful, Practical. Simple or they won’t start it, lean or they won’t finish it, impactful or it’s free online, practical or they drift back. It applies to everything: the program, its structure, every session, every worksheet
A resourceA worksheet, guide, checklist or tool. The standard is one simple worksheet and one Principle Card per session. Anything more has to earn its place. Built later, one or two sessions ahead of your client
The skeletonEverything above, on paper: modules with their outcomes, units, key ideas and weeks. This is what the session produces
