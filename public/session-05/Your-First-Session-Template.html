Your First Session

Good enough shipped beats great unshipped.

The floor — clear it and stop

  1. One objective, in one sentence
  2. Three to five key points
  3. One exercise they do
  4. One action they leave with

Once these four exist, this session is finished. Not when it feels finished — when it clears the floor. You will improve it after you have delivered it, based on what actually landed with a real person. Never before, based on how it feels.

The shape of a first session. A first session is a welcome, not the heavy work. Slides 1 to 4 bring them in: welcome them, tell them how this works, show them the map, and give them the success factors. Slides 5 to 9 are the one piece of work it carries. Your best material belongs later — trying to fit it in here is what makes a first session hard to build.
Slide 1

Welcome

Open by acknowledging the decision they made. They have just spent real money on something they have been circling for a while. Two or three lines is plenty.
Worked example

Welcome — I'm really glad you're here.

You've been thinking about this for a long time, and last week you did something about it. That is the hardest part, and it is behind you.

Over the next ten weeks we are going to get your evenings back.

Yours
Slide 2

How this works

The practicalities, in one place, so nothing is a question later. How often, how long, what happens between sessions, how they reach you, and what you need from them.
Worked example

We meet every Tuesday at 4pm, for fifty minutes, on Zoom.

Between sessions there is one piece of work — never more than an hour.

You can email me any time and I answer within a working day.

What I need from you: turn up, do the one thing, and tell me honestly when you haven't.

Yours
Slide 3

The map

Your modules, in order, and what changes at the end of each. People relax enormously when they can see the whole road. You built this in Session 04 — it already exists, so bring it across.
Worked example

1 · Where your time actually goes — you'll know, on paper, by the end of week two.

2 · The commitments running your week — one gets renegotiated.

3 · Protecting the space — it survives a bad week.

4 · Making it permanent — you leave with a system, not a burst of effort.

Yours
Slide 4

What makes this work

The success factors, and what tends to derail people. Giving them both on day one is a gift, and it sets every expectation you need set. Three of each is plenty.
Worked example

What makes clients succeed here: they protect the hour, they do the one thing rather than all of it, and they say when something isn't working.

What derails people: waiting until they feel ready, trying to do everything at once, and going quiet when they've missed a week.

If you go quiet, I'll chase you. That's part of what you're paying for.

Yours
Slide 5

Today's objective

One sentence. What is different for them by the end of this hour? Not what you will cover — what changes.
Worked example

By the end of today you will have named the three commitments that are quietly running your week, and chosen one to renegotiate.

Yours
Slide 6

The key points

Three to five. No more. Each one is something they need to understand for the objective to land. Write the point, and one line on why it matters to them.
Worked example

1 · Most overcommitment is inherited, not chosen — you agreed to it in a different season of life.

2 · The cost is never the hour itself; it is the recovery time either side of it.

3 · Renegotiating is a conversation, not an apology, and it usually goes better than expected.

Yours
Slide 7

The exercise

One thing they actually do — in the session or straight after. Doing, not reading. This is usually the part they remember, and it is where the change happens.
Worked example

Map last week hour by hour on one page. Mark every block you would not have agreed to if asked today. Circle the one that costs the most recovery time.

Yours
Slide 8

Your action this week

One thing they leave with, and they should be able to say it out loud. A date attached wherever it fits.
Worked example

Before we speak again, have the conversation about the circled block. One conversation, not three. Tell me the date you will have it by.

Yours
Slide 9

Close

Thirty seconds. Read the action back, have them say it in their own words, and say when you next speak.
Worked example

So: one conversation, by Thursday, about the Wednesday evening block. Say that back to me.

Good — I'll see you next Tuesday.

Yours

What you do not need

A workbook. A handout pack. A recording. Sessions two through eight. A name for your methodology. Any of it can come later, and most of it never needs to. Some of the best coaching sessions ever delivered were a worksheet and a conversation.

Delivering it

This page is enough to run the session from. Print it, put it beside you, and talk. If you would rather have something on screen, one slide per heading above is exactly right — and the narrator rule applies to you too: teach over the slide, never read it.
